Scope: 2018-2026. Compiled 2026-09-20 for the 30-minute talk "Beyond Dispensing: How Supply Chain Data Can Predict and Prevent Patient Dropout," Informa Connect IRT & RTSM 2026 (Oct 6-7, 2026, Hyatt New Brunswick, NJ). Each finding is tagged [Evidenced], [Vendor/marketing], or [Secondary/aggregator] where the distinction matters for crediting on a slide.

## Key Question 1: What have IRT & RTSM (Informa), CTS Europe/East/West, GCSG, DIA, SCOPE, DPHARM, CNS Summit, and OCT covered on retention, dropout prediction, patient-centric supply / DTP, or joining IRT with other systems?

### Takeaway
On the confirmed 2026 IRT & RTSM agenda, "Beyond Dispensing" is the only session that mentions patient dropout or retention; five other 2026 sessions cover IRT-to-eClinical integration and data strategy without touching retention, which is the space the talk should explicitly bridge. Across the wider circuit, DTP-as-retention-lever has been a stock claim at GCSG and CTS since 2020, and SCOPE 2024 (Tufts/Getz) is where the "dropout doubled 2010-2020" statistic was presented.

## Key Question 2: Which sponsors have publicly described clinical supply "control towers," integrated data platforms, or patient-level supply visibility, and did any mention retention or dropout?

### Takeaway

**AbbVie + Medidata (Acorn AI), AAIC 2021** [Evidenced: conference poster]
- Poster: "Identifying Predictive Factors of Patient Dropout in Alzheimer's Disease Clinical Trials." Authors: Patricia J. Allen, Iman Abba, Corinne Ahlberg, Luke Benz, Hiuyan Lau, Jingshu Liu, Fareed Melhem (Acorn AI by Medidata, a Dassault Systemes company); Nahome Fisseha, Hana Florian (AbbVie Inc.). Presented at AAIC, July 26-30, 2021, Denver and online. Copyright AbbVie Inc. 2021. — [Poster PDF](https://www.medidata.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AAIC-2021-Identifying-Predictive-Factors-of-Patient-Dropout-in-Alzheimers-Disease-Clinical-Trials.pdf)
- Data: 8,103 patients from 665 sites in 7 completed Phase 3 oral AD trials; mean treatment period 1.4 +/- 1.6 years; dropout rate 21.2 +/- 10.8%. — [Poster PDF](https://www.medidata.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AAIC-2021-Identifying-Predictive-Factors-of-Patient-Dropout-in-Alzheimers-Disease-Clinical-Trials.pdf)
- Method: multivariate Cox proportional hazards models predicting dropout (and non-AE/death dropout) within 3, 6, and 12 months of randomization; ROC-AUC >= 0.60; probability of dropout "typically overestimated," PR-AUC < 0.24. — [Poster PDF](https://www.medidata.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AAIC-2021-Identifying-Predictive-Factors-of-Patient-Dropout-in-Alzheimers-Disease-Clinical-Trials.pdf)
- Predictors included operational site features: "size of clinical staff" (estimated from the average number of Medidata Rave users per study at a site; HR 1.01, p=.02), low surrounding population density (HR 10.50, p=.04), patient age (HR 1.07, p=.005), race (HR 3.60, p=.04), anxiety history (HR 1.44, p=.006) and anxiety AE during trial (HR 1.54, p=.04). Conclusion: "A limited set of patient and site features can be used to estimate patient dropout risk within 3, 6, and 12 months of randomization" and "important interactions exist between patient and site factors." — [Poster PDF](https://www.medidata.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AAIC-2021-Identifying-Predictive-Factors-of-Patient-Dropout-in-Alzheimers-Disease-Clinical-Trials.pdf)

## Key Question 3: Have TransCelerate, CTTI, or the IRT/clinical-research community (ACRP, SCT, "IRT Compendium") published on retention monitoring or dispensing data as an operational signal?

### Takeaway
TransCelerate and CTTI both address retention, but as patient-experience and recruitment-planning frameworks (P-PET, SPFQ, Personalized Clinical Trials Framework; CTTI 2016 recruitment recommendations), not as operational monitoring; nothing from either consortium was found that treats dispensing or supply data as a retention signal. ACRP published a 2023 retention piece (Medable author) built on CISCRP burden data.

### Cited Findings
- TransCelerate Patient Experience Initiative: Patient Protocol Engagement Toolkit (P-PET), Study Participant Feedback Questionnaire (SPFQ; administered at beginning, during, and end of study), and the statement that "improved patient experiences lead to increased study participation and reduced dropout rates." — [TransCelerate Patient Experience](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/initiatives/patient-experience/); [P-PET](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/assets/patientexperience/patient-protocol-engagement-toolkit/)
- TransCelerate Personalized Clinical Trials Framework: stated aim to "potentially increase recruitment into trials and reduce study participant dropout rates." — [Personalized Clinical Trials Framework](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/assets/personalized-clinical-trials-framework/)
- Peer-reviewed description of the TransCelerate toolkits (2020): "Amplifying the Voice of the Patient in Clinical Research: Development of Toolkits for Use in Designing and Conducting Patient-Centered Clinical Studies." — [PMC7704503](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7704503/)
- CTTI "Recommendations: Planning for Successful Trial Recruitment" (May 2016): recruitment considerations to begin at protocol design with evidence-based feasibility; performance monitoring included. Retention addressed as a planning outcome, not as a monitoring method. — [CTTI Recruitment Recommendations PDF](https://ctti-clinicaltrials.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/CTTI_Recruitment_Recs.pdf); [CTTI Recruitment project](https://ctti-clinicaltrials.org/about/ctti-projects/recruitment-2/)
- CTTI diversity recommendations include "data-driven strategies" to "monitor recruitment and retention of diverse participants." — [CTTI Diversity Recommendations](https://ctti-clinicaltrials.org/access/ctti-recommendations-increasing-diversity-in-clinical-trials/)
- ACRP, Feb 22, 2023: "Unique Considerations for Patient Retention in Decentralized Clinical Trials," Ingrid Oakley-Girvan (Medable): 30% average dropout; nearly one-quarter of enrolled participants do not complete (CISCRP 2019/2021 surveys, 5,500+ respondents); 44% found travel burdensome in 2021 (up 15 points from 2019); 40% cited visit length; $6,533 average recruitment cost per participant (re-cited). Proposes a three-phase engagement model. — [ACRP](https://acrpnet.org/2023/02/22/unique-considerations-for-patient-retention-in-decentralized-clinical-trials)
- Vendor framing of IRT data as a supply forecast input (not a retention signal): Signant Health blog states RTSM/IRT systems aggregate "historical enrollment patterns, screen failure rates, patient dropout trends, and site activation timelines to generate accurate supply forecasts." [Vendor] — [Signant Health blog](https://signanthealth.com/resources/blog/clinical-trial-supply-chain-management-rtsm-irt)
- Mosio explainer: "more advanced IRTs can use an algorithm to predict when a study drug supply will run low based on patient enrollment and drop out rates." [Vendor explainer] — [Mosio](https://www.mosio.com/irt-clinical-trials/)

### Inferences
- The consortium literature runs the causal arrow one way only: dropout rates feed supply forecasts. No consortium or vendor document found runs it the other way (supply/dispensing events feed dropout risk). This supports the talk's claim of novelty.

### Gaps

## Key Question 4: Analyst and consultancy commentary on retention costs, cost of a lost patient, and cost of replacing a dropped patient (McKinsey, Deloitte, IQVIA Institute, ZS, Tufts CSDD)

### Takeaway
The commonly quoted "$6,533 to recruit, $19,533 to replace" figures trace to a 2015 Forte Research Systems infographic and are re-cited by vendors and CROs; they are not in the Fogel 2018 review that some pages cite for them. The evidenced numbers to use are Tufts CSDD/Getz (dropout +105.1% 2010-2020; ~30% of enrolled participants drop out) and the Medidata dropout calculator's timeline rule of thumb, while the phase-specific per-patient loss ranges ($30K-$300K) are unsourced vendor estimates.

### Cited Findings

**Tufts CSDD (Getz)** [Evidenced]
- Dropout rates rose 105.1% between 2010 and 2020; enrollment duration (FPFV-LPLV) rose 36.9%; presented at SCOPE 2024 (March 19, 2024). — [Clinical Trial Vanguard](https://www.clinicaltrialvanguard.com/conference-coverage/tufts-csdd-new-insights-on-the-clinical-trial-industry/)
- Tufts CSDD is routinely cited for "30% of those who agree to participate in a trial will drop out." [Secondary re-citation] — [IntuitionLabs patient retention overview](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/patient-retention-clinical-trials)
- Getz on complexity: more fragmentation, customization and moving parts lead to longer trials and "greater likelihood of patient dropout." — [BioSpace interview with Ken Getz](https://www.biospace.com/outdated-clinical-trial-approaches-leading-to-high-failure-rate)

**Cost to replace a dropped patient** [Origin is marketing collateral; flag on slide]
- mdgroup (Oct 1, 2025), "The True Cost Of Patient Drop-outs In Clinical Trials": "$6,533 to recruit one patient" and "$19,533 to recruit a new patient if one is lost due to non-compliance," attributed to PubMed 30264133 (Fogel 2018). Also: ~30% dropout (attributed to PMC3684189); 80% of trials delayed at least a month (attributed to PMC7673977); delays cost "$600,000 (and potentially as high as $8 million) per day"; per-patient loss ranges Phase 2 $30K-$70K, Phase 3 $75K-$150K+, rare disease $150K-$300K+ (no source given). — [mdgroup](https://mdgroup.com/blog/the-true-cost-of-patient-drop-outs-in-clinical-trials/)
- Verification: the full text of Fogel DB, "Factors associated with clinical trials that fail and opportunities for improving the likelihood of success: A review," Contemporary Clinical Trials Communications, 2018 (PMC6092479) does NOT contain $6,533 or $19,533. Its reference 75 is "Lopienski K. 2015. Retention in Clinical Trials - Keeping Patients on Protocols" (Forte Research Systems infographic, June 1, 2015), which is the probable origin of both figures. Fogel does report: patients who described site visits as stressful were more than twice as likely to drop out (38% of dropouts vs 16% of completers); 21% of withdrawals in one trial were due to "symptom burden"; early dropouts were twice as likely to find the ICF hard to understand (35% vs 16%); one UK study found only 31% of 114 trials met enrollment goals. — [Fogel 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6092479/); [Forte infographic (origin, 2015)](https://forteresearch.com/news/infographic/infographic-retention-in-clinical-trials-keeping-patients-on-protocols/)
- The $19,500 figure is re-cited without further sourcing by Scout Clinical, Octalsoft, Woodley, and the SCOPE-adjacent Cognivia collateral. [Secondary] — [Scout Clinical PDF](https://scoutclinical.com/hubfs/Web-Insights%202024/Scout%20-%20Understanding%20Clinical%20Trial%20Patient%20Attrition.pdf?hsLang=en); [Octalsoft](https://www.octalsoft.com/clinical-trial-patient-attrition-causes-impact-on-research-success/); [Woodley](https://woodleytrialsolutions.com/news/how-to-reduce-costly-delays-in-clinical-trials)
- Tufts-attributed daily cost of recruitment/retention issues "up to $37,000 per day" and "approximately 29% of a clinical trial's costs" attributed to enrollment; both appear only in secondary aggregators. [Secondary, unverified] — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/patient-retention-clinical-trials)

- Positions dropout as "the measurable cost of burden placed on patients and sites"; calculations use "industry-standard benchmarks and assumptions" and are "for illustrative purposes"; rule of thumb: every 10% of dropout adds a 45-day replacement delay (30-day screening window plus 15 days coordination/restart); site-density multiplier on baseline dropout from 0.92x (very high density) to 1.10x (very low density). — [Medidata Patient Dropout Calculator](https://www.medidata.com/en/patient-dropout-calculator/)

## Key Question 5: What has been published about direct-to-patient (DTP) shipping and home dispensing improving retention, with numbers?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidenced source is the Tufts CSDD survey published in DIA Global Forum (Nov 2025): 90% of participants with DTP experience said it positively impacted their ability to stay enrolled and 60% of sites said DTP positively affected enrollment and completion, but these are perceptions, not measured dropout rates. Retention percentages attached to DTP/decentralized models (Science 37's 82-98%; the "96% vs ~70%" comparison) come from vendor case studies with unnamed sponsors.

### Cited Findings
- Tufts CSDD, DIA Global Forum, November 2025 (Florez, Harper, Ford, Getz): two cross-sectional surveys; 301 participant responses (166 with DTP experience) and 160 site responses (112 with DTP experience). Participants: 93% found DTP more convenient; 86% more likely to participate in future trials offering DTP; 90% said DTP positively impacted their ability to stay enrolled/retention; 98% had issues resolved when they occurred. Sites: 87% receptive to DTP; 68% still preferred traditional in-person delivery; 61% observed positive impact on willingness to enroll; 60% said DTP positively impacted enrollment and completion rates. The study did not measure actual dropout. [Evidenced survey; perception-based] — [DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/november-2025/direct-to-patient-dtp-shipments-of-investigational-treatments-in-clinical-trials-participant-and-site-receptivity/)
- IntuitionLabs "Patient-Centric RTSM" article: a 2020 Phase 4 breast cancer study by "a top pharma sponsor" with Science 37, fully decentralized (telemedicine, home nursing, ePRO), RTSM coordinating randomization and direct home shipments, "96% patient retention rate, roughly a 30% improvement over traditional site-based oncology trials where retention is ~70%." Sponsor unnamed; primary Science 37 case study for a breast cancer Phase 4 could not be located, and Science 37's own 96% figure appears to relate to a lupus/Sjogren's study. [Secondary; provenance unclear; do not cite as breast cancer without confirming] — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/patient-centric-rtsm-enhancing-clinical-trial-experience)
- Science 37 case studies (sponsors not named; all Direct-to-Patient Site model with IMP shipped to home): PBC 82% retention; Parkinson's 98%; Narcolepsy 98% visit completion; Fragile X 94%; CNS/depression 97.1%; COVID-19 prophylaxis >90% visit completion. [Vendor case studies] — [Science 37 case studies](https://www.science37.com/resources/case-studies)
- 4G Clinical, "Direct to Patient (DTP) Clinical Trials Knowledge Sharing Series" white paper (RTSM vendor; content not fetched). [Vendor] — [4G Clinical DTP white paper PDF](https://www.4gclinical.com/hubfs/Direct-to-Patient_Clinical_Trials_WP_US-1.pdf?hsLang=en-us)
- World Courier "The DtP model": states "85% of trials fail to retain sufficient patient numbers, and the average dropout rate is 30%" and that travel is a primary driver, which DTP addresses. [Vendor; numbers re-cited] — [World Courier](https://www.worldcourier.com/insights/the-direct-to-patient--model)
- DHL DtP collateral: DTP "reduce[s] the need for on-site visits and improve[s] patient retention." [Vendor claim, no data] — [DHL DtP PDF](https://www.dhl.com/content/dam/dhl/global/csi/documents/pdf/glo-csi-clinical-logistics-direct-to-patient-final.pdf); [DHL DtP page](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-hk/ship-with-dhl/services/direct-to-patient-clinical-trials)
- SCRS, "Redefining Access in Clinical Trials Through Direct-to-Patient IMP Shipping" (site-society perspective; not fetched). — [SCRS](https://myscrs.org/resources/redefining-access-in-clinical-trials/)
- Trials@Home RADIAL proof-of-concept trial (IMI consortium): "The Supply of Investigational Medicinal Product and Management of Study Materials for Decentralized Participants - Insights from the Trials@Home RADIAL Proof-of-Concept Trial," Heath et al., Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics, 2025. Page returned 403; this is the most rigorous public-consortium DTP supply paper and worth pulling manually for IMP-supply and retention detail. [Evidenced; not read] — [Wiley CPT](https://ascpt.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cpt.70072)
- Clinical Leader summary of a Tufts CSDD analysis finding "substantial net benefits" to decentralized trials (not fetched). — [Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/tufts-finds-substantial-net-benefits-to-using-decentralized-trials-0001)

### Inferences
- There is no published controlled comparison of retention with and without DTP shipping in an otherwise identical trial; every retention number attached to DTP is either a survey perception (Tufts/DIA 2025) or a single-arm decentralized case study from a DCT vendor. The talk can state this plainly and position dispensing/shipment data as the way to actually measure it.
- The DTP shipment record itself (address confirmation, delivery confirmation, missed delivery, reschedule) is an unexploited operational signal; none of the DTP sources treat shipment events as a retention indicator.

- The 4G Clinical DTP white paper and SCRS DTP piece were not read.
