# Early dropout / retention-risk detection in the eClinical stack (EDC, CTMS, RBQM, clinical analytics)

Scope: how vendors, CROs and sponsors detect participant retention / dropout risk early from operational eClinical data (EDC, CTMS, RBQM, IRT/RTSM, eCOA). Period 2016-2026. Marketing claims are flagged as such; evidenced results are flagged with sample sizes and venue. Several primary vendor/trade pages (Applied Clinical Trials, Medidata "Patient Burden Index" article, PHUSE 2025 RBQM paper, Ripple Science blog) returned HTTP 403/503 during this research and could only be read via search snippets; those are marked "(snippet only)".

## KQ1. Which vendors/CROs market predictive dropout or retention-risk models or dashboards, what inputs do they use, and what evidence do they cite?

### Takeaway
Very few eClinical vendors market a *patient-level* predictive dropout model with published performance. The dominant pattern is (a) site-level retention KRIs and trial-level QTLs inside RBQM platforms (CluePoints, Medidata, Veeva CTMS RBSM, Remarque), (b) enrollment/site-performance forecasting sold as "AI" (Medidata Intelligent Trials, PPD TrueCast, Fortrea Xcellerate, IQVIA OCT Planning Suite) that does not claim dropout prediction, and (c) a small set of niche vendors (Cognivia Signal, Ripple Science) and academic groups (TrialBench; medRxiv attrition study) that do claim dropout prediction with stated accuracy but with limited or unverifiable validation detail.

**Academic / benchmark evidence on ML dropout prediction (trial-level, not patient-level)**
- TrialBench (Chen, Sun, Zitnik, Fu; arXiv 2407.00631v2, 3 Sep 2024) includes a patient-dropout-rate prediction task over 62,058 trials using eligibility criteria, disease codes, drug SMILES, text and MeSH features; baseline multimodal DL ROC-AUC 0.72 (Ph I), 0.73 (Ph II), 0.73 (Ph III), 0.67 (Ph IV). This predicts trial-level dropout *rate* at design time, not which enrolled patient will drop out. — [TrialBench arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00631v2)
- A medRxiv preprint (Nov 2021) models attrition drivers across "1,325 trials representing one million patients" (sponsor-independent dataset). Full text was 403; details unverified. — [medRxiv 2021.11.12.21266277](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.12.21266277v1.full)
- Clinical (non-trial-ops) ML dropout literature: tree-based models (random forest, XGBoost with SMOTE) performed best for psychotherapy dropout; predictors were younger age, unemployment, comorbidity, no partner. Illustrates method but not eClinical operational inputs. — [PMC13469726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13469726/)

### Inferences
- The "inputs" question is mostly answered by the KRI layer rather than by ML: EDC-derived visit compliance (missed assessment rate, off-schedule visit rate), early-termination status, screen failure, query metrics, AE rates. No mainstream EDC/CTMS vendor documents a patient-level model that fuses PRO completion, IRT dispensing, and query burden into a dropout score.
- Claimed accuracies (Cognivia 93%, Ripple 87.5%) are vendor-reported without published methods, base rates or confusion matrices; with ~30% dropout prevalence, "accuracy" is weakly informative.
- Big CROs' AI messaging centers on enrollment forecasting and site selection; retention is addressed via patient-engagement services rather than predictive risk scoring.

### Gaps
- No public documentation found of Medidata Rave/Detect having a patient-level dropout-prediction model; the Patient Burden Index article could not be fetched (403).
- No performance evidence found for Parexel, Syneos, ICON, Fortrea, or IQVIA retention prediction claims.
- Remarque's "patients at risk of discontinuation" claim could not be verified on its site.
- Ripple Science and medRxiv attrition study details unverified (503/403).

## KQ2. Which retention-related KRIs are in common RBQM libraries (TransCelerate, CluePoints, Medidata), how are thresholds set, and are they patient- or site-level?

### Takeaway
Retention appears in every major library as site-level KRIs (early termination rate, screen failure rate, missed assessment rate, off-schedule visit rate) and trial-level QTLs (premature discontinuation, lost to follow-up, withdrawal of consent, missing primary endpoint data). Thresholds are set either statistically (CluePoints: statistical score >1.3, p<0.05 vs study distribution) or from historical benchmarks plus expert judgment (TransCelerate QTL framework); actions are taken at the site level, not the individual patient level.

### Cited Findings

**TransCelerate**
- TransCelerate's Risk Indicator Library contains "more than 140 Risk Indicators," derived from a blinded member survey in Q4 2014. — [TransCelerate news, Nov 2015](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/news-center/11-5-2015-2/)
- TransCelerate's implementation guide lists a "Subject discontinuation" category examining "outliers and trends in ratio of subjects discontinued to subjects that are randomized" and reasons for discontinuation, and a "Subject recruitment" category covering screen failure rate and enrollment rate outliers; other categories are Safety, Data Quality, Issue Management, Investigational Product (accountability, dosing, compliance), Staffing & Equipment, Essential Documents, On-site Workload. Risk indicators are "predominantly used at the patient level" but thresholds trigger actions "predominantly on the site level, and not on the trial level" (remote assessment, site contact, or on-site visit). — [TransCelerate: Risk Indicators and Thresholds](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/rbminteractiveguide/best-practices-for-implementation/risk-indicators-and-thresholds/)
- TransCelerate QTL framework member survey (2021): ~60% of members had implemented the framework; QTL parameters monitored included missing data for primary endpoints, patient lost to follow-up (percentage/number), AE reporting compliance, and premature study discontinuations. — [TransCelerate: Member companies using the QTL framework](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/value-impact/implementation_experiences/member-companies-using-the-qtl-framework/)
- Historical QTL benchmarks (Makowski et al., GSK lead, with Roche, Sanofi, Pfizer, Janssen, AZ, Allergan, Novartis; TIRS, Aug 2021): 294 trials from seven TransCelerate members plus 45 Medidata AD/oncology trials. Mean (median) rates: lost to follow-up 1.78% (0.80%); endpoint data not collected 12.58% (7.90%); ineligible randomized 6.70% (3.21%); premature drug discontinuation 16.03% (10.70%); withdrawal of consent 3.95% (2.90%). Authors advise using historical benchmarks with internal data and protocol-specific expertise rather than universal thresholds; larger trials showed higher premature discontinuation. — [PMC8492573](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8492573/)

**CluePoints**
- Nine "commonly used" KRIs in CluePoints' outcomes study: AE rate (safety); missed assessment rate, off-schedule visit rate, protocol deviation rate (compliance); auto-query rate, query response cycle time, visit-to-eCRF entry cycle time (data quality); early termination rate and screen failure rate (enrollment/retention). Early Termination Rate = number of early-terminated patients divided by post-randomization visits, "designed to identify sites struggling with patient retention." Sites flagged when statistical score >1.3 (p<0.05) versus study-wide distribution; evaluation is at site level. — [de Viron et al., TIRS 2022, PMC9589525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9589525/)
- CluePoints QTL guidance (17 Apr 2025, drawing on RBQMLive 2022 with Steve Young/CluePoints, UCB, Janssen, Pfizer): organizations run "between two and four QTLs per trial, complemented by numerous KRIs to monitor risk at the site level"; QTL parameters include early termination before endpoint milestones, lost to follow-up, premature study-drug discontinuation, missed endpoints/assessments; thresholds set by cross-functional teams (medics, statisticians) from historical databases by TA/phase, scenario analysis, and regulatory expectations. — [CluePoints: QTLs, where are we](https://cluepoints.com/qtls-where-are-we-and-how-much-further-can-we-go/)
- QTL parameter prevalence (snippet only from ACT/CluePoints industry survey): "missed assessment rate" in 57.5% of studies with QTLs, "enrolled but ineligible" 25.6%, "early termination rate" 23.6%, "lost to follow-up rate" 14.2%. — [ACT: QTLs, an updated view of industry trends](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/quality-tolerance-limits-an-updated-view-of-industry-trends)
- Statistical QTL methods review (TIRS, 2024) gives a worked example: Phase III, 300 participants, premature discontinuation historically 4%, QTL set at 12% maximum; notes FDA may not accept results if >20% lost to follow-up in some TAs. — [PMC10850247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10850247/)
- CluePoints says it is building "a core library of advanced/optimized KRIs... likely no more than 15 or so" applicable to most studies (snippet). — [CluePoints RBM implementation Q&A](https://cluepoints.com/cluepoints-answers-your-risk-based-monitoring-implementation-questions/)

**Medidata / Veeva**
- Medidata RBQM 101 and Detect materials define KRIs/QTLs generically; no public retention KRI list. — [Medidata RBQM 101](https://www.medidata.com/en/life-science-resources/medidata-blog/risk-based-quality-management-rbqm/)
- Veeva CTMS RBSM uses the RACT-derived risk library; no published retention KRI definitions. — [Veeva Vault Help RBSM](https://clinical.veevavault.help/en/lr/62567/)

**Early methodological source**
- A KRI-based central statistical monitoring method was developed inside a large ongoing RCT (2011-2012), monitoring e.g., SAE reporting rates and compliance with study treatment/visit timing to identify poor-performing sites. — [PMC3287710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3287710/)

### Inferences
- Retention KRIs in practice are lagging indicators (a patient has already terminated) aggregated per site; leading patient-level signals (visit window drift, missed PRO diary days, dispensing gaps) are captured only indirectly via missed-assessment and off-schedule-visit KRIs.
- The TransCelerate note that indicators are computed at patient level but acted on at site level explains why no library defines a per-patient "dropout risk score."

### Gaps
- The full TransCelerate 140-indicator library text was not retrieved (member resource); only category descriptions are public.
- ACT articles containing the QTL prevalence survey returned 403; figures are from search snippets.
- No public Medidata or Veeva KRI library with retention definitions was found.

## KQ3. Sponsor/CRO case studies of catching dropout early via central monitoring or site-performance analytics

### Takeaway
Named-sponsor, dated case studies specifically about catching dropout early are scarce; the strongest evidence is aggregate and vendor-led (CluePoints' 212-study analysis showing early-termination-rate signals improve after intervention, and TransCelerate's anonymized QTL survey reporting detection of unreported premature discontinuations).

### Cited Findings
- de Viron et al. (CluePoints; TIRS, Oct 2022): 212 studies, 1,676 sites, 23 sponsors, 11 TAs. Across nine KRIs, 82.9% of signals improved by statistical score and 81.1% by observed value; per-KRI statistical-score improvement ranged from 23.1% (off-schedule visit rate) to 86.2% (V2ECT); observed-value improvement 23.6% (AE rate) to 92.8% (V2ECT). Data-quality KRIs improved far more than enrollment/retention and safety KRIs; authors note cumulative KRI design constrains measurable improvement for some metrics and that "improved quality does not come automatically." — [PMC9589525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9589525/)
- Earlier CluePoints analysis (20 Apr 2022, de Viron & Steijn): 144 V2ECT signals, 97.2% improved statistically; missed assessment rate signals improved in 89.7% of cases, AE reporting rate 76.5%. No early-termination KRI analyzed. — [CluePoints: Does central monitoring lead to higher quality](https://cluepoints.com/does-central-monitoring-lead-to-higher-quality-an-analysis-of-kri-outcomes/)
- CluePoints SDM analysis: 159 studies, 1,111 at-risk sites, 23 organizations; 83% of sites with statistical data monitoring improved data-inconsistency scores vs 56% comparison; 46% vs 17% quality improvement. Data quality, not retention. — [ACRP, Steve Young, 13 Jun 2024](https://acrpnet.org/2024/06/13/does-risk-based-quality-management-rbqm-actually-improve-quality); [PMC11043176](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11043176/)
- TransCelerate 2021 member survey (companies anonymized): QTL monitoring "detected unreported premature discontinuations," identified AE reporting gaps and data-entry backlogs, and early detection of QTL breaches "enabled timely remedial actions" (e.g., missing primary-endpoint data in acute indication studies). — [TransCelerate QTL implementation experiences](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/value-impact/implementation_experiences/member-companies-using-the-qtl-framework/)
- ACT "RBQM Implementation Journey: A Case Study" retrospectively applied KRIs to a completed sponsor study to test whether RBQM would have enabled "early detection of factors driving study failure" (snippet only; article 403; sponsor not named in snippet). — [ACT case study](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/rbqm-implementation-journey-case-study)
- IQVIA (2021) cites a home-based follow-up example retaining 61 participants (25% of enrollees) who would otherwise have withdrawn; this is an intervention outcome, not an analytics detection case. — [IQVIA blog](https://www.iqvia.com/blogs/2021/03/can-decentralized-clinical-trials-solve-attrition)
- Cognivia Signal: T1D validation, 93% accuracy claim (vendor, Oct 2025). — [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/cognivias-ai-software-predicts-probability-of-trial-patient-dropping-out/)

### Inferences
- Publicly, "catching dropout early" is evidenced at the site-signal level (a site's early-termination KRI signal improving after action), not at the level of individual patients rescued.
- The weaker improvement for enrollment/retention KRIs vs data-quality KRIs in the 212-study analysis suggests retention signals are harder to remediate once triggered, supporting the case for leading indicators.

### Gaps
- No case study naming a sponsor, year, and quantified dropout reduction attributable to central monitoring was found in accessible sources; ACT case-study pages were blocked (403).
- Searches for Pfizer/AZ/Bayer/Novartis retention-KRI case studies returned only protocol PDFs and conference listings (e.g., SCOPE Europe 2022 RBQM track: https://www.scopesummiteurope.com/22-1/risk-based-monitoring).

## KQ4. What ICH E6(R3), FDA guidance, and TransCelerate say about monitoring participant retention as a quality risk

### Takeaway
ICH E6(R3) (final, 6 Jan 2025) does not name retention as a KRI but embeds it in QbD/RBQM: sponsors set trial-level "quality tolerance limits" on critical-to-quality factors, monitors must review and report "participant recruitment and retention rates," investigators must try to learn withdrawal reasons and avoid loss of collected data, and unnecessary participant burden must be avoided. FDA's 2023 RBM Q&A reinforces continuous risk assessment; TransCelerate's QTL framework explicitly lists lost-to-follow-up and premature discontinuation as QTL parameters.

### Cited Findings
- ICH E6(R3) 3.10.1.3 Risk Control: "the sponsor should set pre-specified acceptable ranges (e.g., quality tolerance limits at the trial level) to support the control of risks to critical to quality factors... Where deviation beyond these ranges is detected, an evaluation should be performed to determine if there is a possible systemic issue and if action is needed." — [ICH E6(R3) Step 4 final, Jan 2025](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- ICH E6(R3) 3.11.4.2 Centralised Monitoring: evaluation of accumulated data by qualified persons (medical monitor, data scientist/data manager, biostatistician); "centralised data analytics can help identify systemic or site-specific issues, including protocol noncompliance and potentially unreliable data" and "may support the selection of sites and/or processes for targeted site monitoring." — [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- ICH E6(R3) monitor activities include "(h) Reviewing and reporting the participant recruitment and retention rates" and identifying "missing data, inconsistent data, data outliers, unexpected lack of variability and protocol deviations." — [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- ICH E6(R3) 2.9.1-2.9.2 (investigator): for participants who did not reach routine end of trial, follow protocol instructions "to avoid loss of already collected data... loss of already collected data may bias results"; the investigator "should make a reasonable effort to ascertain the reason(s)" for withdrawal and may explain "the value of continuing their participation to minimise trial participants withdrawal," without undue influence. — [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- ICH E6(R3) principles: processes "proportionate to the risks to participants and to the importance of the data collected and that avoids unnecessary burden on participants and investigators"; sponsor "should not place unnecessary burden on participants and investigators." Sponsor must describe how participant data are handled on withdrawal/discontinuation. — [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- Secondary commentary: E6(R3) "includes expanded content supporting the management of critical activities, such as randomisation, blinding/masking and participant retention, with reference to ICH E8(R1)." — [CT Toolkit summary](https://www.ct-toolkit.ac.uk/news/summary-key-changes-ich-e6-r3-guidelines); [CluePoints: Decoding ICH E6(R3)](https://cluepoints.com/decoding-ich-e6r3-what-it-means-for-risk-based-quality-management-rbqm/)
- FDA final guidance "A Risk-Based Approach to Monitoring of Clinical Investigations: Questions and Answers" (April 2023; correction May 2023) expands the 2013 RBM guidance; themes include developing the monitoring strategy, "risk assessment as a continuous process," and root cause analysis. It does not specifically address retention KRIs. — [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/04/12/2023-07687/a-risk-based-approach-to-monitoring-of-clinical-investigations-questions-and-answers-guidance-for); [FDA guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/121479/download)
- TransCelerate: QTL framework parameters include lost to follow-up and premature discontinuations; RBM methodology position paper published 2013 with a >140-indicator library including subject discontinuation ratio. — [TransCelerate QTL experiences](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/value-impact/implementation_experiences/member-companies-using-the-qtl-framework/); [TransCelerate Risk Indicators](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/rbminteractiveguide/best-practices-for-implementation/risk-indicators-and-thresholds/)
- Regulatory tolerance example cited by statisticians: FDA "may not accept results if more than 20% of patients are lost to follow-up" in certain TAs (as reported in the QTL methods review; not a direct FDA citation). — [PMC10850247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10850247/)

### Inferences
- E6(R3) frames retention as a critical-to-quality factor to be controlled via QTLs and monitored via centralised analytics; this creates a regulatory hook for patient-level dropout-risk tooling but does not require it.
- The explicit investigator duty to explore withdrawal reasons (2.9.2) implies value in surfacing at-risk participants *before* withdrawal so that conversation can happen.

### Gaps
- No FDA text was located that specifically discusses retention/dropout as a monitoring KRI; only the general 2023 RBM Q&A.

## KQ5. Documented integration of IRT/RTSM dispensing data into RBQM or central monitoring for patient-level risk

### Takeaway
IRT/RTSM vendors document EDC/CTMS/eCOA integrations and drug-accountability/compliance tracking, and TransCelerate's library has an "Investigational Product" KRI category (accountability, dosing, compliance), but I found no public documentation of an RBQM platform ingesting IRT dispensing data to compute patient-level dropout risk.

### Cited Findings
- TransCelerate risk indicator categories include "Investigational Product (accountability, dosing, compliance)" alongside subject discontinuation. — [TransCelerate Risk Indicators](https://www.transceleratebiopharmainc.com/rbminteractiveguide/best-practices-for-implementation/risk-indicators-and-thresholds/)
- ICH E6(R3) 3.10.1.1 lists "randomisation... investigational product administration" among processes whose risks must be identified. — [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)
- Suvoda documents two-way integrations with EDC, CTMS, lab and other systems, and dispensing/accountability/compliance monitoring functions; no RBQM/KRI feed described. — [Suvoda systems integration](https://www.suvoda.com/systems-integration); [Suvoda RTSM](https://www.suvoda.com/rtsm-irt-clinical-trial)
- IRT is described (trade source) as tracking dispensing by patient allocation, auto-flagging non-protocol entries, and enabling "remote, site-level monitoring of drug accountability logs." — [Clinical Leader: Drug Accountability electronic system](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/drug-accountability-electronic-system-0001)
- YPrime frames E6(R3) as expecting IRT platforms to provide "end-to-end visibility into trial activities" and risk management traceability; no RBQM data feed. — [YPrime on E6(R3) and IRT](https://www.yprime.com/what-the-ich-e6r3-guidelines-mean-for-irt-companies-today/)
- Oracle Clinical One pushes subject screened/randomized status changes to Veeva CTMS (enrollment status, not dispensing). — [Oracle Digital Gateway](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/life-sciences/clinical-one/digitalgateway-guide/veeva-vault-ctms.html)
- Third-party overview: RBQM platforms calculate KRIs/QTLs from "integrated data feeds from EDC, CTMS, lab systems, and ePRO"; IRT is listed as part of the digital backbone but not as a KRI source. — [IntuitionLabs RTSM/EDC integration](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/rtsm-edc-integration-clinical-trials); [SRM Tech blog](https://www.srmtech.com/knowledge-base/blogs/phases-of-clinical-trials-explained-building-a-digital-backbone-across-phases-i-iv/)
- Medidata offers RTSM within the same platform as Rave and Detect (data harmonization across sources is a Detect claim), but no document ties RTSM dispensing to retention risk. — [Medidata Detect fact sheet](https://www.medidata.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Medidata-Detect-Fact-Sheet-Aug-23.pdf)

### Inferences
- Dispensing gaps (missed dispensing visits, late kit returns, unused-drug returns) are a natural leading indicator of disengagement, but published RBQM practice treats IRT data as a compliance/accountability source at site level, not a patient retention signal. This is a plausible white space.

### Gaps
- No vendor (CluePoints, Medidata, Remarque, Veeva, Oracle) publicly documents IRT dispensing-based patient-level risk indicators; the PHUSE 2025 paper "RBQM in Clinical Data Management" (lexjansen.com/phuse-us/2025/ar/PAP_AR03.pdf) could not be fetched (DNS failure) and may contain relevant data-source lists.
