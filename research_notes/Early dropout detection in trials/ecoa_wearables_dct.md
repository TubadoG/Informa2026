# Early Dropout Detection via eCOA/ePRO, Wearables, DCT Platforms and Engagement Apps

Scope: how digital data streams (diary/ePRO completion, app logins, wear-time, medication-dosing confirmation) act as leading indicators of disengagement and withdrawal in clinical trials and remote digital studies; what vendors offer; what published evidence exists; and whether acting on early signals improves retention. Window 2015-2026. Evidence tier is flagged on every finding: [PEER-REVIEWED], [CONFERENCE ABSTRACT], [VENDOR/MARKETING], [PRESS/SECONDARY].

---

## Key Question 1: What engagement metrics serve as dropout precursors, and how far ahead do they signal withdrawal?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced precursors are (a) early adherence in the first 1-2 weeks (medication dosing, diary/survey completion, first-week wear or step volume), (b) a widening gap between sessions or a 2-week inactivity window, and (c) response latency to notifications. In remote studies the majority of attrition happens inside the first week, so the useful "lead time" is often days, not months; in longer site-based or DCT studies, a 2-4 week window of declining activity precedes formal dropout and is detectable (one platform found ~72% of dropouts cut activity by more than half in their final 2 weeks).

### Cited Findings

**Retention curves and where the drop happens (remote/digital studies)**
- Pratap et al. 2020 (npj Digital Medicine) pooled 8 remote digital health studies, >100,000 participants, ~3.5M remote assessments. Median retention across studies was only 5.5 days; per-study medians: Brighten 26 d, Asthma 12 d, MyHeart Counts 9 d, ElevateMS 7 d, mPower 5 d, Phendo 4 d, START 2 d, SleepHealth 2 d. "More than half of the participants discontinued participation within the first week." [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Pratap 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026051/); [npj version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-0224-8)
- Same paper: five engagement clusters over an 84-day window. Cluster C5 "abandoners" (about 1 day of use) was the largest group at a median 54.6% of participants; "dedicated users" (96.4% daily use) were a median 9.5%. Retention drivers: clinician referral +40 days median retention (44 d vs 4 d self-referred); compensation +22 days (one study, Brighten); having the target clinical condition +7 days (13 d vs 6 d); age 60+ +4 days; sex not significant (P = 0.3). [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Pratap 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026051/)
- Lancet Digital Health (Dec 2024/Jan 2025) secondary analysis of two large observational digital studies: BIL study 5,784 participants / 388,600 daily surveys (mean 67 per person) and an Evidation Health study 89,479 participants / 2,080,992 daily surveys (mean 23 per person). Unsupervised clustering + survival + multistate modelling. Age was the most consistent factor: 18-29 year-olds concentrated in low/medium adherence clusters and dropped out sooner; 60+ over-represented in high-adherence clusters. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Lancet Digital Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(24)00219-X/fulltext); [PubMed 39722250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39722250/)
- RADAR-MDD (npj Digital Medicine 2023): 614 depression participants followed up to 2 years across UK/Spain/Netherlands. 67.6% remained engaged after 43 weeks. Three engagement subgroups by clustering. Passive data outlasts active data: 44.6% of those who stopped completing surveys after 8 weeks kept sharing passive Fitbit data for an average of 42 more weeks. The least-engaged group had PHQ-8 scores ~4 points higher, took 3.8 hours longer to respond to notifications, and were ~5 years younger. Retention was also associated with smartphone ownership/brand and recruitment site. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Zhang et al. 2023, KCL Pure](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/long-term-participant-retention-and-engagement-patterns-in-an-app/); [npj DOI 10.1038/s41746-023-00749-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-023-00749-3)
- Brighten (JMIR 2025 "Dynamic indicators"): 2,193 enrolled in two remote smartphone RCTs; 1,093 completed at least one post-baseline questionnaire. Clinical trajectory was a dynamic indicator: PHQ-9 "improvers" had 50.7% mean completion vs 42.6% for non-improvers (p<.001); hazard ratio for retention 0.78 (95% CI 0.67-0.91), improvers stayed ~7.4 days longer. Elastic-net prediction of completion category gave F1 0.66-0.72 within cohort but only 0.32-0.46 across cohorts, i.e. models did not generalise. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Brighten dynamic indicators, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12721583/); [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00942)
- mPower (Parkinson's, ResearchKit): 6,805 completed enrollment, 1,087 self-reported PD. Secondary analysis found prior professional diagnosis and high task performance scores encouraged continued use, while disease progression hindered app usage. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [mPower Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201611); [Emerald ITP analysis](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/itp-07-2019-0366/full/html)
- Apple Heart Study (419,297 enrolled): loss to follow-up was estimated from monthly survey response rather than passive watch data; among 2,161 notified participants, 63.7% completed the 90-day survey and 43% the end-of-study survey, vs 70.2% of unnotified participants completing end-of-study. The data-management lessons paper says the team did NOT formally model dropout predictors, but recommends collecting reasons for non-adherence and tracking timestamped event sequences to monitor progression. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Apple Heart Study lessons, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9378511/)
- Heartline (J&J/Apple, age 65+): 34,244 enrolled, 32,299 started (94%), 28,386 completed 1-year (88%), 21,649 completed 2-year (67%), ~6% formal withdrawals. Weekly app engagement 96.0% at 30 days, 92.8% at 1 year, 85.7% at 2 years; ePRO completion 89.9% baseline, 81.8% at 12 months, 76.3% at 24 months. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Heartline experience, JACC Advances / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12539446/)

**Wearable wear-time as a signal**
- COVID-RED DCT (17,825 participants, Ava bracelet worn nightly + app sync): 80.4% retained at 6 months, 68.5% at completion. Multivariable logistic regression identified age >50 (23.6% vs 16.6% dropout), pensioner/rentier status (28-29% vs 16.8% for full-time workers), living alone, and vaccination status as discontinuation predictors. The team explicitly used bracelet compliance of 30-70% as the trigger band for a "high-risk" flag. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [COVID-RED retention, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12514562/)
- Cedars-Sinai EHR-connected wearables (JMIR 2025): 8,616 patients, engagement defined as daily step-count reporting to the EHR; median engagement 21 months (IQR 9-38); 68% still engaged past 1 year. First-week step volume predicted disengagement: <5,000 median daily steps in week 1 carried higher disengagement risk (HR 0.79 for 5,000-7,499 steps, 0.72 for >10,000 steps vs the low-step reference); ages 18-34 had the highest disengagement hazard. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [JMIR 2025 wearable survival analysis, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12489408/)
- Actigraphy wear compliance across 5 neurological trials (143 patients, GT9X Link, Koneksa repository): median compliance 89% in month 1, 88% over 3 months, 84% at one year; "wear compliance tends to decrease with time, but even at the one-year mark, median compliance remained >80%." Authors propose compliance-by-day as a threshold for analysis-set inclusion. [CONFERENCE ABSTRACT] — [MDS Abstracts](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/actigraphy-wear-compliance-across-cns-disorders/)
- 12-month nurse smartwatch study (298 enrolled, 273 = 92% completed): average wear-time 77.4% (SD 32.6), stayed above a 70% threshold in every month; participants reporting discomfort had 9.1% lower wear-time and those calling the device intrusive 6.9% lower. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC11829698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11829698/)
- Breast-cancer fitness-tracker studies report compliance falling to 17.5% by day 180 of a six-month follow-up (cited in an npj Parkinson's Disease 2025 review of wrist-worn compliance). [PEER-REVIEWED, secondary citation] — [npj Parkinson's Disease 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01016-w)

**App inactivity gaps / login cadence**
- LIVA Healthcare lifestyle platform (JMIR 2019): 2,684 patients; dropout defined as 4 consecutive weeks with no platform action (chosen because <5% re-engage after 4 idle weeks). Random forest AUC 0.92, accuracy 86%, precision 89%; logistic regression AUC 0.84; decision tree 0.82. Strongest predictors: intervention provider, a 2-week inactivity gap, and number of coach advices. "71.77% of dropouts decreased activity by >50% in final 2 weeks," i.e. dropout is gradual and detectable ~2 weeks ahead. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Pedersen et al. JMIR 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6753691/)
- Pratap 2020 cluster definitions use inter-session gap as the discriminator: moderate users median 2-day gap, sporadic users median 5-day gap, before the abandoner cluster. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Pratap 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026051/)

**Medication adherence technology as a dropout predictor**
- AiCure (CNS Spectrums, two Phase II trials: schizophrenia NCT03351244 n=235 and attenuated psychosis syndrome NCT03230097): first 2 weeks of app-confirmed dosing predicted average adherence for the rest of the trial; adherence in the latest 4 weeks predicted probability of premature dropout. With a 0.6 adherence cutoff, 7-, 10- and 14-day windows flagged 22%, 20% and 19% of participants as high-risk with 81%, 90% and 96% confirmation of subsequent non-adherence (14-day window had the lowest false-omission rate). eCRF reported 78% compliance where AiCure showed 26% in one cohort. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [CNS Spectrums](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cns-spectrums/article/aibased-adherence-prediction-for-patients-leveraging-a-mobile-application-to-improve-clinical-trials/DC2E0B8A27D2AD5BD8EBED6C85151264)
- AiCure's own blog claims early termination fell to 7.8% among adherent participants across 28 Phase I-IV trials (>4,000 patients, 43 countries) using its platform plus central monitoring, and cites a Boehringer Ingelheim pilot partnership. [VENDOR/MARKETING, no peer-reviewed comparator] — [AiCure blog](https://aicure.com/blog/how-ai-can-help-reduce-attrition-rates-in-randomized-controlled-trials); [AiCure asthma blog](https://www.aicure.com/blog/how-predictive-drug-adherence-can-forecast-patient-dropout-in-asthma-clinical-trials)
- Non-digital corroboration of the "week-1 adherence" rule: in a 5-week light-therapy RCT (n=93, 20% dropout), participants completing <80% of prescribed week-1 dose had a 5.85-fold higher dropout risk (95% CI 1.41-24.21), independent of age, sex, arm, expectations and adverse events. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC9397075](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9397075/)
- Smart-pill-bottle literature (MS virtual RCT, myeloma/lenalidomide, HIV, AF) frames the devices as adherence monitors/intervention tools; I found no study using smart-cap data as a *trial dropout* predictor. — [MS virtual trial, J Neurol Sci](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022510X21003063); [Myeloma SPB, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31663462/)
- emocha video DOT: mean adherence 94% (median 96%) across >1,400 videos; validated in TB, HCV, OUD; CDC-recognised as DOT. No dropout-prediction analysis. [VENDOR page summarising studies] — [emocha evidence](https://emocha.com/evidence-based-medication-adherence-interventions/)
- Medisafe: MedISAFE-BP RCT (JAMA IM 2018) tested effect on adherence/BP, not trial retention. [PEER-REVIEWED, not a dropout study] — [JAMA IM](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2678454)

**Non-behavioural predictors (for context)**
- Big-Five personality scores at wave 1 predicted later-wave attrition (17.6% attrition across 2,697 questionnaires): negative likelihood ratio 0.333, NPV 0.933, described as "fair" performance. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC9471583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9471583/)

### Inferences
- The consistent "early window" across modalities is 7-14 days: AiCure dosing (14-day best), LIVA app inactivity (2-week gap), light-therapy week-1 adherence, first-week step count (Cedars), and Pratap's first-week cliff. A practical rule for a dropout radar: score risk at day 7 and day 14, then re-score on any 14-day activity decline of >50%.
- Passive data (wear/sync) is a lagging, not leading, indicator relative to active data: RADAR-MDD participants kept sharing Fitbit data for ~42 weeks after they stopped surveys. Missed ePRO therefore fires before wear-time decline; wear-time decline is closer to formal withdrawal.
- Response latency to notifications (RADAR-MDD +3.8 h in least engaged) is an under-used, cheap signal that most eCOA systems already timestamp.
- Younger age is the single most replicated demographic risk factor in remote studies (Pratap, Lancet DH, RADAR-MDD, Cedars); COVID-RED is the exception where older, retired participants dropped more, likely device/tech burden.

### Gaps
- No study I found reports a formal "lead time" distribution (days from first missed diary to withdrawal) in a regulated Phase II/III eCOA trial; the evidence is from observational digital studies and one lifestyle platform.
- Apple Heart Study and All of Us have not published participant-level dropout prediction models.
- Full text of the Lancet Digital Health 2024 paper was paywalled to my tools; effect sizes beyond the age finding are not captured.

## Key Question 2: What do eCOA/DCT vendors offer as compliance or engagement alerts, and what evidence do they cite?

### Takeaway
Nearly every eCOA/DCT vendor now ships threshold-based compliance alerts, site dashboards and escalating reminders; only a few (Datacubed, ObvioHealth, AiCure) publish retention numbers, and those are internal case studies or small single-arm datasets, not controlled comparisons. Predictive (ML) dropout scoring is mostly marketing language rather than published validation.

### Cited Findings
- **Clario (ex-ERT)**: real-time reporting "automatically alerts sites when patient compliance is below a set threshold"; email alerts to sites on specific patient-reported events; cites ePRO vs paper compliance of 94% vs 11% (the classic Stone 2002 paper-diary finding). [VENDOR/MARKETING; the 94/11 figure is peer-reviewed but not about alerts] — [Clario: Is ePRO more than a questionnaire](https://clario.com/resources/articles/is-epro-more-than-just-a-questionnaire/); [Clario eCOA vs paper](https://clario.com/resources/articles/ecoa-vs-paper-which-should-i-use-for-my-upcoming-trial/)
- **Signant Health**: positions strength in rater training/CNS; its resources discuss why wearables remain underused (charging, wearing, troubleshooting burden). No published retention-lift data found. [VENDOR] — [Signant wearables article](https://signanthealth.com/resources/why-are-wearables-and-sensors-still-underused-in-clinical-trials); [Signant ePRO modality guide](https://signanthealth.com/resources/choosing-the-right-epro-modality)
- **YPrime**: launched eCOA "Participant Hub" (April 2026) with a "For You" page centralising visit schedules and task tracking, explicitly pitched against the ~30% average trial dropout rate. No outcome data. [PRESS] — [GlobeNewswire April 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/07/3269076/0/en/yprime-expands-ecoa-platform-with-participant-hub.html); [YPrime engagement page](https://www.yprime.com/patient-engagement/)
- **Kayentis**: Clin'form includes real-time dashboards, alerts and consistency checks. No retention evidence found. [VENDOR] — [Kayentis single-device article](https://kayentis.com/epro-and-ediary-what-about-using-a-single-device/); [IntuitionLabs eDiary guide](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ediary-clinical-trials-best-practices)
- **Medable**: platform lists reminders, real-time notifications "to ensure compliance", TeleVisits, wearables; claims reduced burden and increased retention without published figures. [VENDOR] — [Medable eCOA/ePRO](https://www.medable.com/platform/ecoa-epro)
- **ObvioHealth**: internal "meta-analysis" of 6 fully decentralized BYOD studies (752 enrolled, 658 completed, 4-19 months, mean 10.5 months): 88% retention and 90% weighted ePRO compliance vs a claimed 70% site-based benchmark; one study with its COACH virtual support team reached 89% retention / 93.5% ePRO compliance. Attributes success to "a user-friendly app, automatic notifications and a live virtual clinical support team." Authors Dallabrida and Culberth-Notaro; not peer-reviewed. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [ObvioHealth meta-analysis page](https://www.obviohealth.com/resources/meta-analysis-epro-compliance-and-retention-for-patients-using-their-own-smartphones-in-fully-decentralized-clinical-trials); [ObvioGo ePRO](https://www.obviohealth.com/dct-platform/epro)
- **Datacubed Health (Linkt)**: internal case study, N=36 (32 analysed), 12-week open-label trial with 5 remote assessments: 89% overall compliance, 95% among retained, 88% Kaplan-Meier retention (28/32). Sites with full staff training hit 95.5% compliance vs 80.7% at sites with implementation gaps (p=0.02). [VENDOR case study] — [Datacubed case study](https://www.datacubed.com/retention-and-compliance-case-study/). A separate peer-reviewed randomized modality study (284 participants, 24 weekly surveys over 6 months) found the Datacubed app beat paper on retention (P<.001) and beat all modalities on adherence (vs DCH web P=.02; vs third-party web P<.001; vs paper P<.001); biweekly payment beat bulk payment for retention (P=.001). [PEER-REVIEWED] — [JMIR 2025, PMC11967695](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967695/)
- **THREAD**: publishes tool lists for retention (reminders, televisits, engagement content); no quantified retention claims found. [VENDOR] — [THREAD retention tools](https://www.threadresearch.com/resources/decentralized-clinical-trial-tools-that-increase-patient-retention)
- **Science 37**: "hundreds of decentralized studies"; no retention outcome data surfaced in this search. [PRESS] — [PRNewswire market report](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/insights-on-the-virtual-clinical-trials-global-market-to-2027---key-players-include-dassault-systemes-medable-science-37-and-thread-301708969.html)
- **Koneksa**: dashboards "monitor device wear, questionnaire completion, and participant compliance without code"; its data repository underpinned the MDS actigraphy compliance abstract (5 trials, 143 patients, >80% median compliance at 1 year). [VENDOR + CONFERENCE ABSTRACT] — [Koneksa](https://www.koneksahealth.com/); [MDS abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/actigraphy-wear-compliance-across-cns-disorders/)
- **ActiGraph**: CentrePoint Insight Watch launch (wear detection, cloud sync). No retention evidence. [PRESS] — [Applied Clinical Trials](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/actigraph-launches-new-centrepoint-insight-watch)
- **AiCure**: see KQ1; the peer-reviewed CNS Spectrums prediction work is the strongest vendor-linked evidence; the 7.8% early-termination figure is a blog claim. — [CNS Spectrums](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cns-spectrums/article/aibased-adherence-prediction-for-patients-leveraging-a-mobile-application-to-improve-clinical-trials/DC2E0B8A27D2AD5BD8EBED6C85151264); [AiCure blog](https://aicure.com/blog/how-ai-can-help-reduce-attrition-rates-in-randomized-controlled-trials)
- **DCT platform landscape**: Everest Group's 2023 DCT Platforms Provider Compendium (licensed to DTRA) profiles Medable, Science 37, THREAD, Curebase, Castor, ObvioHealth etc. as offering reminders/engagement modules. [ANALYST REPORT] — [Everest/DTRA compendium PDF](https://www.dtra.org/hubfs/Everest%20Group%20-%20Decentralized%20Clinical%20Trial%20Platforms%20Provider%20Compendium%202023.pdf?hsLang=en)
- Industry blog claims that operationalising "ePRO drift, sync gap, reschedule rate, negative sentiment" with 12-48 hour SLAs yields "10-18% absolute retention lift in pragmatic pilots." No data or citation behind it. [MARKETING/UNSOURCED] — [CCRPS blog](https://ccrps.org/clinical-research-blog/predicting-patient-dropout-how-ai-will-solve-clinical-trial-retention-by-2026)

### Inferences
- Vendor alerting is almost universally *threshold* based (compliance % below X, diary missed by Y days), not *trajectory* based (rate of decline, latency drift). The published predictors point to trajectory features as the better signal.
- The vendor retention numbers (88-89%) are single-arm and from short, low-burden, BYOD or open-label studies; they should be presented as "what good looks like" rather than as evidence that alerts cause retention.

### Gaps
- No vendor I found publishes a controlled before/after or A/B evaluation of its compliance alerts on withdrawal rates.
- Curebase, Castor, Empatica, Fitbit/Google and Apple ResearchKit did not surface retention-alert evidence in this search; treat as unknown rather than absent.

## Key Question 3: What is the evidence that retention interventions work, and which ones?

### Takeaway
The Cochrane evidence (Brueton 2013; Gillies 2021, 81 trials, >100,000 participants) supports only monetary incentives with any confidence (RR ~1.18-1.20 for questionnaire return), and rates all evidence low-to-very-low; electronic reminders, SMS, telephone follow-up and personalised prompts show RRs near 1.0. Outside Cochrane, targeted telephone outreach (All of Us: OR 2.0; COVID-RED: dropout 11.4% vs 20.3%) and structured multi-channel engagement programmes (Heartline: 86% weekly engagement at 2 years) show the strongest real-world signals, but are non-randomised.

### Cited Findings
- **Gillies et al. 2021 Cochrane (MR000032.pub3)**: 70 papers / 81 retention trials, >100,000 participants, 53 UK. Monetary incentives vs usual follow-up RR 1.20 (95% CI 1.06-1.36), low certainty (about 30 vs 25 per 100 retained at low baseline). Electronic reminders RR 1.01 (0.95-1.09, low); SMS/electronic prompts RR 1.03 (0.98-1.08, very low); telephone follow-up RR 1.04 (0.94-1.17, very low); personalised prompts RR 0.97 (0.89-1.07, low); pens RR 1.02 (1.00-1.05, low); short questionnaires RR 1.01 (0.89-1.14, very low); including a diary RR 0.97 (0.96-0.98, moderate, i.e. adding a diary slightly *hurts* retention). "None of the comparisons are supported by high-certainty evidence"; most outcomes were postal questionnaire return, not clinic attendance. [PEER-REVIEWED SYSTEMATIC REVIEW] — [Gillies 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8092429/); [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.MR000032.pub3/full); [PubMed 33675536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33675536/)
- **Brueton et al. 2013/2014 Cochrane and BMJ Open**: 38 trials; monetary incentive vs none for postal questionnaires RR 1.18 (95% CI 1.09-1.28, P<0.0001); offer of monetary incentive also improved electronic questionnaire return. Six strategy classes: incentives, communication, questionnaire format, case management, behavioural, methodological. [PEER-REVIEWED SYSTEMATIC REVIEW] — [Cochrane MR000032.pub2](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.MR000032.pub2/full); [PubMed 24496696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24496696/)
- **Recruitment SWATs rarely measure retention**: Arundel and Clark 2024 found only 10 eligible recruitment SWATs that also assessed retention. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Clinical Trials 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005310/)
- **Trial Forge / UK TMN priority SWATs** list "most effective way of using participant reminders" and "flexibility" and "financial incentives" as top unanswered retention questions. — [UK TMN priority list](https://www.tmn.ac.uk/resources/recruitment-and-retention-swat-priorities); [Trial Forge PRESS1 protocol](https://www.trialforge.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PRESS1_Protocol_v1_15.05.2024.pdf)
- **SWAT86 pre-notification**: 83% valid response with pre-reminder vs 79% without; OR 1.28 (95% CI 0.67-2.42), not significant. [PEER-REVIEWED SWAT] — [PubMed 41908263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41908263/)
- Recent SWATs on thank-you cards (SWAT 119) and holiday cards embedded in trials are published 2025-26; effect sizes not captured here. — [SWAT 119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41913257/); [Holiday card SWAT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42157274/)
- **All of Us non-randomised intervention (PLOS One 2024)**: 17,593 intervention vs 47,832 control participants at 50 sites (Apr-Aug 2020). Telephone appointment arm (n=6,253): adjusted OR 2.01 (95% CI 1.81-2.23) for follow-up survey completion; combination phone+postal (n=5,400) OR 1.91 (1.66-2.20); postal alone (n=5,940) OR 0.92 (0.79-1.07), not significant; overall completion only 10.4%. [PEER-REVIEWED, non-randomised] — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0308995)
- All of Us also reports that compensation increased task completion (context-dependent), and that 6 years in, only 34% of under-represented participants vs 54% of represented participants had completed an activity in the prior 18 months. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [All of Us Participant Labs, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179602/)
- **COVID-RED signal-triggered outreach** (see KQ1): vaccinated pensioners with 30-70% bracelet compliance were called; dropout among called participants was 11.4% vs 20.3% in the same stratum before the intervention and 14.8% in the full population in the same period. Also used dynamic compliance-based email schedules with gold/silver/bronze medals and behavioural alerts to inactive participants. Non-randomised. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [COVID-RED, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12514562/)
- **Heartline engagement programme**: escalating reminders (in-app, email 1 week and 48 h before ePRO expiry, push 48 h before), points rewards, Stand Ring challenges, ongoing education. Exit survey (15,783 completers, 73% response): 68.4% rated research participation "extremely motivating", 67.8% rated points "very effective", 66.6% rated notifications "very effective." No control arm for the engagement programme itself. [PEER-REVIEWED, descriptive] — [Heartline, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12539446/)
- **Payment cadence**: biweekly payment beat bulk end-of-study payment for retention (P=.001) in the 284-person modality RCT. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [JMIR 2025, PMC11967695](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967695/)
- **Pratap 2020**: clinician referral (+40 d) and compensation (+22 d) were the two largest retention levers in remote studies, suggesting the human relationship at entry matters more than in-app nudges. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Pratap 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026051/)
- **JMIR scoping review (2022) of enrolment/retention in remote digital studies**: texting and email reminders were the most common retention tactics; community co-design and partnerships were associated with high retention. [PEER-REVIEWED REVIEW] — [JMIR 10.2196/39910](https://doi.org/10.2196/39910)
- **Nurse smartwatch study**: newsletters, quarterly town halls, personalised data summaries, 18-hour email support (1.27-day mean resolution), charging stations and spare bands on units; 92% completion at 12 months. [PEER-REVIEWED, single-arm] — [PMC11829698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11829698/)
- **Economic evaluations of retention SWATs** are rare; a 2023 systematic review found few with cost data. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Gkekas et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/26320843221147838)

### Inferences
- Untargeted reminders do not move retention (Cochrane RR ~1.0), but *targeted human contact triggered by a risk signal* does in every non-randomised evaluation found (COVID-RED, All of Us phone arm). The distinction between blanket nudges and signal-triggered outreach is the key slide message, with the caveat that no RCT has yet tested signal-triggered outreach.
- Money works modestly and reliably; how it is paid (biweekly vs lump sum) may matter as much as how much.
- Adding a diary reduces retention (Cochrane RR 0.97, moderate certainty), so ePRO burden itself is a dropout driver; the alert system should also feed protocol-burden decisions.

### Gaps
- No randomised evaluation of an engagement-signal-triggered intervention (alert -> site call) on withdrawal was found in 2015-2026 literature.
- No evidence found on transport support or stipends as *triggered* interventions; Cochrane covers incentives only as blanket offers.
- Treweek-led Trial Forge work on retention is mostly priority-setting and protocols (PRESS1) rather than results as of this search.

## Key Question 4: Is there evidence that early disengagement signals can be operationalised into a working "dropout radar" (models, thresholds, performance)?

### Takeaway
Published models reach AUC 0.84-0.92 when trained on within-platform behavioural features (inactivity gaps, coach interactions), and simple rules (adherence <60% over 14 days; <80% week-1 dose; 2-week >50% activity drop) identify 19-22% of participants as high risk with 81-96% precision; but generalisation across cohorts is poor (Brighten F1 fell to 0.32-0.46), so models need per-study recalibration.

### Cited Findings
- LIVA random forest AUC 0.92 / accuracy 86% / precision 89% with 2-week inactivity as a top feature. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [JMIR 2019, PMC6753691](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6753691/)
- AiCure 14-day adherence window at 0.6 cutoff: 19% flagged, 96% confirmed non-adherent. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [CNS Spectrums](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cns-spectrums/article/aibased-adherence-prediction-for-patients-leveraging-a-mobile-application-to-improve-clinical-trials/DC2E0B8A27D2AD5BD8EBED6C85151264)
- Brighten elastic net: within-cohort F1 0.66-0.72, cross-cohort 0.32-0.46. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC12721583](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12721583/)
- Light-therapy trial: week-1 adherence <80% -> 5.85x dropout risk. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC9397075](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9397075/)
- COVID-RED: logistic-regression risk strata plus a compliance band (30-70%) used prospectively to target calls. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC12514562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12514562/)
- Cedars-Sinai: first-week step count as a passive early predictor of long-term wearable disengagement (Cox HRs 0.72-0.79 for higher-step groups). [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC12489408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12489408/)
- Personality (Big Five) at baseline gave NPV 0.93 but only "fair" discrimination; useful as a static prior, not a trigger. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [PMC9471583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9471583/)
- A 2005 US patent (7,415,447) already describes decision rules that treat "patterns of non-compliance data" as leading indicators of later, more serious non-compliance and trial failure, evidence that the concept predates current ML framing. — [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7415447)

### Inferences
- A defensible slide-ready rule set: (1) day-7 and day-14 adherence <60-80% depending on modality; (2) any 14-day window with >50% drop in activity; (3) notification response latency drifting upward; (4) survey non-completion while passive data continues (a "silent disengagement" state that RADAR-MDD shows lasts ~42 weeks and is the ideal outreach window).
- Expect to flag roughly one in five participants; precision in the 80-95% range is achievable within a study but cross-study transfer is unproven, so a radar should ship with per-study thresholds and a feedback loop.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head of threshold rules vs ML models on withdrawal in regulated trials.
- No published evidence yet on LLM/sentiment-based signals (e.g. negative sentiment in support chat) as dropout predictors; only blog claims.
